# VERSE EXPERIENCE

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Approved for Design Exploration
**Owner:** Product & Design
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

# Vision

Verse is the heart of Antar.

Everything else in the product exists to help the reader arrive here with presence and leave with greater understanding.

Verse is not simply where scripture is displayed.

It is where wisdom is encountered.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of the Verse experience is to help a reader engage deeply with a single teaching.

Verse creates an environment where reading, understanding, and reflection happen naturally without distraction.

It does not attempt to teach on behalf of the reader.

It creates space for personal understanding.

---

# Why This Exists

Modern software encourages speed, interruption, and constant switching.

Wisdom requires the opposite.

Verse exists because reading sacred text deserves an environment designed specifically for contemplation.

Rather than maximizing engagement, Verse maximizes presence.

---

# Experience Philosophy

Verse should feel like opening a thoughtfully designed book.

The interface should gradually disappear until only the teaching remains.

Every visual, interactive, and technical decision should answer one question:

> Does this help the reader engage more deeply with the teaching?

If the answer is no, it does not belong.

---

The intended journey through Verse is:

Arrive

↓

Orient

↓

Read

↓

Understand

↓

Reflect

↓

Carry Forward

↓

---

# Experience Goals

The Verse experience should:

* help readers focus on one teaching at a time,
* encourage thoughtful reading rather than consumption,
* provide understanding without replacing personal interpretation,
* invite reflection without requiring it,
* and leave readers carrying one meaningful idea into the rest of their day.

---

# Emotional Arc

The intended emotional progression is:

Curiosity

↓

Presence

↓

Understanding

↓

Reflection

↓

Clarity

↓

Continuation

The interface should quietly support this progression without calling attention to itself.

---

# Experience Responsibilities

Verse is responsible for:

* presenting scripture clearly,
* supporting understanding,
* preserving focus,
* encouraging reflection,
* creating emotional continuity,
* providing a natural path to continue reading.

---

# Non-Responsibilities

Verse is not responsible for:

* social interaction,
* discussions,
* comments,
* likes,
* streaks,
* gamification,
* recommendations,
* trending content,
* community features,
* or AI-first interpretation.

Those belong elsewhere—or not at all.

---

# Information Architecture

The reading experience follows this information hierarchy:

1. Scripture
2. Transliteration (optional)
3. Primary Translation
4. Secondary Translation (optional)
5. Understanding
6. Reflection
7. Saar
8. Continue Reading

No supporting feature should appear before scripture.

---

# Reading Flow

The reader should naturally progress through four stages:

## Read

Encounter the verse without interruption.

---

## Understand

Read the translation and supporting explanation.

---

## Reflect

Pause and consider the teaching personally.

Reflection is always invited.

Never required.

---

## Carry Forward

Read Saar.

Leave the experience with one clear thought worth remembering.

# Interaction Principles

Reading is the primary interaction.

Everything else supports it.

Readers should never feel pressured to:

* open AI,
* journal,
* bookmark,
* share,
* or continue immediately.

All supporting actions should remain invitations.

Never obligations.

---

# AI Philosophy

AI exists to support understanding.

It does not replace reading.

It does not replace contemplation.

It does not become the teacher.

Readers should first encounter the teaching directly before choosing to engage with AI.

The Bhagavad Gita remains the primary voice.

AI remains secondary.

---

# Reading Preferences

Future versions may allow readers to customize:

* text size,
* line spacing,
* reading margins,
* translation,
* transliteration,
* appearance,
* and reading comfort.

Customization should improve understanding rather than personalize the teaching itself.

---

# States

The Verse experience should support:

* Loading
* Offline
* Reading
* Completed
* Bookmarked
* AI Unavailable
* Translation Unavailable

Transitions between states should remain calm and unobtrusive.

---

# Accessibility

Verse should remain fully usable with:

* Dynamic Type,
* VoiceOver,
* TalkBack,
* keyboard navigation,
* reduced motion,
* high contrast,
* multilingual typography,
* and offline reading.

Accessibility adaptations should preserve the emotional experience rather than creating a separate version of the product.

---

# Motion

Motion should reinforce continuity.

Transitions should feel like turning the next page of a book rather than moving between applications.

Stillness is preferred whenever animation does not improve understanding.

---

# Engineering Responsibilities

The Verse experience should:

* support offline reading,
* separate content from presentation,
* preserve reading progress,
* support multiple translations,
* allow future AI providers,
* remain resilient to missing data,
* and expose reusable subcomponents.

The experience should remain composable without tightly coupling unrelated responsibilities.

---

# Analytics Philosophy

Analytics exist to improve the experience—not to optimize engagement.

Appropriate measurements include:

* reading completion,
* reflection usage,
* accessibility feature usage,
* offline usage,
* translation preferences.

The product should avoid metrics that encourage addictive behavior.

Success is measured by quality of engagement rather than quantity.

---

# Success Criteria

The experience succeeds if:

* the scripture remains the visual focus,
* readers immediately know where to begin,
* understanding feels supportive rather than overwhelming,
* reflection feels natural,
* AI remains secondary,
* readers never feel rushed,
* readers leave carrying one meaningful idea.

---

# Failure Modes

The experience fails if:

* AI becomes more prominent than scripture,
* readers skip directly to AI every session,
* the interface feels crowded,
* interaction interrupts reading,
* reflection feels like homework,
* Saar becomes repetitive,
* navigation competes with the teaching,
* or visual design becomes more memorable than the wisdom itself.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

* advertisements,
* recommendations,
* comments,
* likes,
* streaks,
* achievement systems,
* countdown timers,
* pop-ups during reading,
* decorative illustrations,
* excessive iconography,
* AI presented before scripture,
* unnecessary notifications,
* and any interaction that competes with contemplation.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may explore:

* additional translations,
* richer commentary,
* seasonal reading themes,
* adaptive reading preferences,
* audio reading,
* guided study,
* deeper cross-references,
* and personalized reflection history.

Future evolution should never compromise the simplicity of the core reading experience.

---

# Design Decisions

### AI appears after personal engagement.

**Reason**

Readers should first encounter the teaching directly.

AI exists to deepen understanding—not replace first-hand reading.

---

### Reflection is optional.

**Reason**

Transformation cannot be forced.

Readers should always feel invited rather than obligated.

---

### Saar concludes every reading.

**Reason**

Readers benefit from leaving with one memorable insight that bridges reading and daily life.

---

### Supporting actions remain visually quiet.

**Reason**

The scripture should always remain the center of attention.

---

# Open Questions

The following require validation through design and user testing:

* When should Saar first appear?
* Should Reflection precede Saar or follow it?
* How should AI reveal itself without becoming distracting?
* Should Reading Progress remain visible throughout the experience?
* How should Verse adapt on tablets?
* Which reading preferences belong in Version 1?

---

# North Star

When someone closes the Verse experience, they should not remember the interface.

They should remember one teaching that quietly changes how they move through the rest of their day.
