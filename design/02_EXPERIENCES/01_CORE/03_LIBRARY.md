# LIBRARY EXPERIENCE

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Approved for Design Exploration
**Owner:** Product & Design
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

# Vision

Library is where curiosity meets intention.

It helps readers discover the teachings of the Bhagavad Gita with confidence, clarity, and calm.

Rather than overwhelming readers with endless choice, Library gently guides exploration while preserving the structure and integrity of the text.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of Library is to help readers confidently discover what they want to read next.

Whether someone is beginning for the first time, returning after a break, or searching for a specific teaching, Library should make discovery feel effortless.

---

# Experience Promise

Library promises:

> **"I will help you discover the teaching you need without overwhelming you with choice."**

It does not attempt to maximize exploration.

It helps readers find a meaningful place to begin.

---

# Primary User Question

> **What should I explore next?**

Every feature within Library should help answer this question.

If something creates more confusion than confidence, it does not belong.

---

# Why This Exists

The Bhagavad Gita is a structured work.

Its chapters build upon one another and together form a complete philosophical journey.

Many readers, however, approach the text with different intentions.

Some wish to begin from Chapter 1.

Others want to revisit a familiar teaching.

Some are searching for guidance on a particular aspect of life.

Library exists to support each of these intentions without compromising the integrity of the original text.

---

# Experience Philosophy

Library should feel like walking through a thoughtfully organized library rather than scrolling through an endless content feed.

Discovery should feel intentional.

Readers should feel invited to explore, never pressured to consume.

The interface should create confidence rather than curiosity for curiosity's sake.

---

Readers should never feel lost or overwhelmed.

Library should:

---

# Experience Responsibilities

Library is responsible for:

* presenting the complete structure of the Bhagavad Gita,
* helping readers discover teachings,
* supporting search,
* preserving continuity,
* and providing clear paths into reading.

---

# Non-Responsibilities

Library is not responsible for:

* trending content,
* popularity rankings,
* recommendations based on engagement,
* community activity,
* achievement systems,
* advertisements,
* or maximizing time spent browsing.

Library exists to begin reading—not replace it.

---

# Information Architecture

The Library experience prioritizes information in this order:

1. Continue Reading
2. Search
3. All Chapters
4. Explore by Theme (Future)
5. Bookmarks (Future)

The original chapter order should remain the primary organizational structure.

Alternative discovery methods should supplement—not replace—it.

---

# Chapter Philosophy

The eighteen chapters are the foundation of the Library experience.

They should always remain accessible in their original sequence.

The application may provide additional paths to discovery, but it should never reorder or obscure the structure of the Bhagavad Gita.

Readers should gradually become familiar with its organization simply through repeated use.

---

# Search Philosophy

Search exists to remove friction.

It should help readers quickly locate:

* chapters,
* verses,
* themes,
* and familiar teachings.

Search should never become the primary method of navigation.

Browsing and understanding the structure of the text remain valuable parts of the experience.

---

# Discovery Philosophy

Discovery should feel intentional rather than algorithmic.

Examples include:

* Continue your current chapter.
* Begin Chapter 1.
* Explore teachings about courage.
* Browse all chapters.

Library should not encourage endless exploration.

Its purpose is to help readers arrive at a meaningful place to read.

---

# Returning Readers

Returning readers should immediately recognize where they left off.

Continue Reading should remain the highest-priority action.

Continuity encourages depth more effectively than constant novelty.

---

# First-Time Readers

First-time readers should feel welcomed rather than overwhelmed.

Library should gently explain the structure of the Bhagavad Gita without becoming instructional.

Readers should understand:

* where to begin,
* how the chapters are organized,
* and that there is no pressure to rush.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

Visual attention should naturally follow this order:

Continue Reading

↓

Search

↓

All Chapters

↓

Supporting Discovery

The original text should always remain visually dominant over supporting features.

---

# Interaction Principles

Exploration should remain:

* calm,
* predictable,
* intentional,
* and focused.

Interactions should encourage readers to enter Verse rather than remain inside Library.

The goal is meaningful discovery—not prolonged browsing.

---

# Motion

Motion should support orientation.

Transitions should communicate movement through a physical collection rather than an endless digital feed.

Animations should remain subtle and preserve context.

---

# Accessibility

Library should remain fully usable with:

* Dynamic Type,
* VoiceOver,
* TalkBack,
* reduced motion,
* high contrast,
* offline mode,
* keyboard navigation where applicable,
* and multilingual typography.

Every reader should be able to confidently discover teachings regardless of ability or environment.

---

# Engineering Responsibilities

Library should:

* preserve canonical chapter ordering,
* support efficient search,
* restore reading progress,
* function offline where appropriate,
* support future discovery methods,
* and remain independent of recommendation engines.

The architecture should separate scripture structure from future personalization features.

---

# Analytics Philosophy

Analytics should improve discoverability rather than increase browsing.

Appropriate measurements include:

* Continue Reading usage,
* successful searches,
* chapter entry rates,
* accessibility feature usage,
* offline discovery.

Avoid optimizing for:

* browsing duration,
* endless scrolling,
* recommendation clicks,
* or content consumption metrics.

The goal is helping readers confidently choose—not keeping them inside Library.

---

# Success Criteria

The Library experience succeeds if:

* readers quickly find what they are looking for,
* exploration feels calm rather than overwhelming,
* chapter order remains intuitive,
* readers naturally enter Verse,
* and repeated use helps readers become familiar with the structure of the Bhagavad Gita.

---

# Failure Modes

The Library experience fails if:

* it resembles a content marketplace,
* recommendations overshadow scripture,
* readers endlessly browse without reading,
* chapter order becomes secondary,
* search replaces thoughtful exploration,
* or the experience rewards novelty over continuity.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

* trending sections,
* popularity rankings,
* recommendation feeds,
* "people also read",
* advertisements,
* achievement badges,
* endless scrolling,
* promotional content,
* feature announcements,
* or algorithmic reordering of scripture.

Library should remain a place of discovery—not consumption.

---

# Future Evolution

Future versions may explore:

* thematic reading paths,
* guided study collections,
* bookmark organization,
* verse comparison,
* advanced search,
* multilingual discovery,
* and personalized reading pathways.

Future additions should always preserve the canonical structure of the Bhagavad Gita.

---

# Design Decisions

### Chapter order is never reordered.

**Reason**

The original structure of the Bhagavad Gita is part of the teaching itself and should remain intact.

---

### Continue Reading remains the primary action.

**Reason**

Helping readers continue meaningful engagement is more valuable than encouraging constant exploration.

---

### Search supports discovery.

**Reason**

Search removes friction without replacing the natural structure of the text.

---

### Discovery remains intentional.

**Reason**

Readers should feel guided toward wisdom rather than drawn into endless browsing.
