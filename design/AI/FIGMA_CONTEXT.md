# Project

Antar

Antar is a contemplative reading experience centered around the Bhagavad Gita.

Its purpose is to help readers engage deeply with scripture, reflect honestly, and gradually build a meaningful relationship with timeless teachings.

Antar is not a meditation app.

Antar is not an AI chatbot.

Antar is not a productivity app.

Antar is not a social network.

Scripture—not the interface—is always the center of the experience.

---

# Product Philosophy

Every design decision should reinforce these principles.

## Scripture before interface

The interface exists to reveal scripture.

It should never compete with it.

---

## Invitation before obligation

Readers are invited to continue their journey.

They are never pressured.

Avoid urgency.

Avoid notifications that create guilt.

Avoid engagement tricks.

---

## Reflection before guidance

Readers encounter scripture first.

Reflection comes next.

Guidance is optional.

AI should never become the primary experience.

---

## Meaning before metrics

Never prioritize:

- streaks
- achievements
- badges
- XP
- percentages
- gamification

Growth is discovered through reflection, not measured.

---

## Silence is a valid outcome

Every interaction does not need a response.

Whitespace is valuable.

Calm is intentional.

Not every screen should feel busy.

---

# Design Personality

Antar should feel:

- calm
- spacious
- intentional
- timeless
- humble
- trustworthy

Antar should never feel:

- addictive
- gamified
- trendy
- noisy
- sales-oriented
- overwhelming

---

# Navigation Philosophy

The experience is intentionally simple.

Home

↓

Today's Invitation

↓

Verse

↓

Reflection

↓

Continue Reading

The interface should encourage readers toward the next meaningful action rather than presenting many competing choices.

---

# Home Primary Action

Today's Invitation is Home's sole primary action.

It presents the next meaningful step based on reader context.

It may represent starting a new reading, continuing an unfinished reading, resuming an unfinished reflection, or an approved curated scripture.

Do not add a separate Continue Journey section unless a later approved decision explicitly requests one.

---

# Component Philosophy

Use only documented components.

Do not invent new reusable UI components unless explicitly requested.

When assembling screens:

Components combine into Compositions.

Compositions combine into Experiences.

Do not flatten these layers.

---

---

# Visual Philosophy

Prioritize:

- whitespace
- hierarchy
- readability
- typography
- rhythm
- consistency

Visual decoration should always remain secondary.

Every screen should feel quiet.

---

# Interaction Philosophy

Every screen should answer one primary question.

Examples:

Home

"What should I do next?"

Chapter

"What should I read?"

Verse

"How do I experience this teaching?"

Journey

"What have I learned?"

Avoid placing multiple competing primary actions on one screen.

---

# AI Guidance Philosophy

Guidance exists to connect readers with scripture.

AI is never the destination.

Never design chat-first experiences.

Never create conversation UIs unless explicitly instructed.

Recommendations should always lead readers into scripture.

---

# Search Philosophy

Search exists for intentional discovery.

Search is not recommendation.

Search should never resemble a social feed.

Readers should understand why results appear.

---

# Journey Philosophy

Journey is memory.

Not measurement.

Never visualize spiritual growth through scores, charts, percentages, streaks, or achievements.

Journey reconnects readers with previous reflections.

---

# Things AI Should Never Generate

Do NOT introduce:

- bottom tab bars unless explicitly requested
- streaks
- achievements
- XP
- meditation timers
- breathing exercises
- mindfulness prompts
- social feeds
- likes
- comments
- popularity indicators
- AI avatars
- chatbot interfaces
- floating action buttons
- carousels
- promotional banners
- advertisements
- onboarding overlays
- notification badges
- confetti
- progress celebrations

Do not redesign Antar into a generic wellness application.

---

# Wireframe Rules

When creating wireframes:

Use grayscale only.

No branding.

No illustrations.

No gradients.

No shadows.

No icons unless placeholders are required.

Focus on:

- hierarchy
- spacing
- layout
- interaction

Beauty comes later.

---

# Screen Generation Rules

Generate only the requested screen.

Do not invent additional screens.

Do not invent navigation.

Do not invent features outside the documented design language.

If information is missing, leave placeholders rather than making assumptions.

---

# Preferred Workflow

1. Read this document.

2. Read the screen-specific prompt.

3. Generate one screen.

4. Wait for human review.

5. Refine iteratively.

Never attempt to design the entire application in one generation.

---

# Definition of Success

A successful design should feel like the interface quietly disappears, allowing the reader's attention to rest on the Bhagavad Gita rather than on the product itself.
