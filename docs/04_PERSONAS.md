# Introduction

Antar is not designed for a demographic.

It is designed for moments in people's lives.

Every person who opens Antar is searching for something.

Sometimes that search is curiosity.

Sometimes it is clarity.

Sometimes it is comfort.

Our responsibility is not to assume why they arrived.

Our responsibility is to meet them where they are.

When designing any feature, imagine a real person sitting beside you.

Ask:

"Would this genuinely help them?"

If the answer is no, redesign it.

# Persona 1 — Aarav

Age: 19

Engineering Student

## About

Aarav recently started college.

His days are busy.

Assignments.

Interviews.

Exams.

Social pressure.

He has heard about the Bhagavad Gita from his parents but never seriously read it.

He wants guidance that feels practical rather than philosophical.

---

## Goals

Reduce stress.

Build discipline.

Understand the Gita without feeling overwhelmed.

Create a healthy morning routine.

---

## Pain Points

Doesn't know where to begin.

Short attention span.

Too much information online.

Feels intimidated by traditional commentaries.

---

## How Antar Helps

Daily five-minute reflections.

Simple explanations.

Personalized guidance.

A calm place before the day begins.

# Universal Needs

Regardless of age,

language,

belief,

or experience,

every Antar user wants:

To feel welcomed.

To feel understood.

To make progress.

To avoid feeling overwhelmed.

To leave calmer than they arrived.

---

# The Empty Chair

Every product discussion includes one imaginary person sitting in the room.

Whenever we debate a feature,

we ask:

Would Aarav understand this?

Would Maya actually use this before work?

Would Meera find this comforting?

Would David feel welcomed?

Would someone having a difficult day feel supported?

If the answer is no,

the feature is not ready.

---

# Design Rule

We never design for "users."

We design for people.
